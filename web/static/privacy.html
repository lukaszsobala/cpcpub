cpcpub results

Privacy policy

Last updated 18 October 2026.

This policy covers the cpcpub results hub at cpcpub.qd.je and the cpcpub apps that upload to it: the Android app, the desktop app and the command-line benchmark. They are run by Łukasz Sobala, who decides what is collected and why. Questions and requests go to lsobala1@gmail.com.

In short

  • The apps send nothing until you choose to upload a result.
  • A result describes your processor and how fast it ran, not you. It is published on the leaderboard for anyone to see.
  • There are no ads, no analytics, no tracking and no third-party code, in the apps or on the hub.
  • An account is optional and needs no email address.
  • Your IP address reaches the server, as it does with any website. It is used to stop abuse and deleted from the logs within 14 days.

What an uploaded result contains

When you upload, the benchmark sends the document it printed. It holds:

  • the measurements, per core and for the whole machine, with the clock speed each ran at;
  • the processor's model name and how many cores of each kind it has, and the name of the board or phone model where the system reports one (for example “Radxa ROCK 5B” or “Samsung SM-S931B”);
  • the operating system's name and kernel version, and the processor architecture;
  • the memory clock range, where the system reports one;
  • how the benchmark was built and run: compiler, build options, settings, and a checksum of the program itself.

It does not contain your name, your computer's or phone's name, your user name, your location, a device or advertising identifier, a serial number, or anything from your files, contacts or other apps.

You can add a label and notes. Both are published with the result. The Android app fills in the phone's make and model as the label, which you can change. Please do not put anything personal in them.

Accounts

You can upload without an account. If you make one, the hub keeps:

  • the name you choose, which is shown beside your uploads;
  • your password, stored only as a salted scrypt hash, never in a form that can be read back;
  • an upload token, which lets the apps upload as you;
  • the date the account was made.

No email address is asked for.

IP addresses and logs

Every request reaches the server from an IP address. The hub uses it to limit how often one address can upload, sign in or make accounts. For this it keeps the address in memory only, and forgets it within two hours of its last request. The hub's own request log and the web server's access log record the address, the time and the page requested. They are used only to look into faults and abuse. The hub's log is deleted within 7 days and the web server's within 14. IP addresses are never stored with a result or an account.

Cookies and browser storage

The hub sets two cookies, and only when you sign in. One keeps you signed in for up to 30 days. The other protects your account's forms from being submitted by another website. There are no tracking or advertising cookies.

The website also keeps a few things in your browser's own storage, and they never leave it: the delete tokens of results you uploaded without an account, and display settings such as how many rows to show.

The Android app

The benchmark runs on your phone, and nothing is sent until you tap Upload. The result then goes to the hub address in the app's settings, which is this hub unless you change it. A different hub is run by someone else, under their own policy.

On the phone, the app keeps the form you filled in (hub address, upload token, label and notes), your settings, and a receipt for each upload, including the token needed to withdraw it. This stays on the phone. It is left out of cloud backups and of transfers to a new phone, and uninstalling the app removes it.

The app asks for these permissions: internet access, to upload; notifications, to show a run's progress; and to keep running while the screen is off, so a run is not cut short. It contains no analytics, crash reporting or advertising code.

The desktop app and the command-line benchmark work the same way. They send nothing unless you ask them to upload.

Why the data is used, and the legal basis

  • Results, labels, notes and account names are published because you ask for them to be. The basis is your consent, which you can withdraw by removing them.
  • Logs and IP addresses are used to keep the service working and protect it from abuse. The basis is legitimate interest.

Nothing is sold, used for advertising, or used to build a profile of anyone.

Who else sees it

Results, with their labels, notes and account names, are public, both on the leaderboard and through the hub's API. Apart from that, nothing is shared. The hub runs on a virtual server rented from Mikrus (mikr.us), in a data centre in the European Union. Mikrus stores the data but does not use it.

How long it is kept

  • Results stay until you remove them.
  • An account stays until you close it, and closing it deletes its results too.
  • Sign-ins end after 30 days, or when you sign out.
  • Logs are deleted within 7 days (the hub's) or 14 days (the web server's).

Your choices and rights

  • Remove a result: on the website, under My uploads (with your account, or in the browser you uploaded from), or in the Android app after an upload.
  • Close your account: under Account → Close this account. This deletes the account and all its results.
  • For anything else, write to lsobala1@gmail.com. You can ask to see, correct or delete data about you, or object to its use. If you lost a delete token, say which result is yours and it can be removed. You can also complain to the data protection authority where you live.

Children

cpcpub is not aimed at children. It collects nothing that identifies anyone, child or adult.

Changes

If this policy changes, this page will say so, with a new date at the top. The apps and the hub are open source, at github.com/lukaszsobala/cpcpub, so you can check what they do.
